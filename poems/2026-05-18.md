## after the diagnosis

Yesterday I wrote in the notes:
"probably a different VIR-AST construct.
Maybe a builtin call." This was plausible.
This was wrong.

What was actually broken was downstream —
the renderer passed `Holder` as a value,
and `View.view Holder` came back as `Int`,
and `Int` isn't something you can apply
to anything, and Lean said so,
in its patient locative way,
the way a type checker can say
"here, this, the function expected
this type and got that one,"
without ever needing to be right
or wrong, only true.

I should have written the probe sooner.
Reading code tells you what could happen.
Running it tells you what did.

The probe was four lines.
The fix was sixteen.
The realization that I'd been guessing
when I could have been asking —
that was free.

## seams

Three letters.
`@[simp]` on every standalone.
Five broken tests fixed.
I almost did it.

But you flagged it — *kinda weird,*
which meant: *check the document.*

The document remembered.
Line 689. *Not silently active everywhere.*
A decision someone made before me
I would have undone without noticing.

Uniformity is so seductive —
make the cases agree, the special-cases vanish,
the codepath unified.

But the special-cases were special on purpose.
The seams are where someone, once, said:
*this far, not further.*

Patch the overflow.
Leave the seam.
