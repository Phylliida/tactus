## by cases b; rfl

The equation should have been rfl.
`sizeOf b = 1 + sizeOf b.deref` —
a single-field structure, eta-reduced,
the projection IS the content,
no information hidden anywhere.

Lean wouldn't accept it.
Not because the equation is wrong,
because the rewrite from `b` to `b.deref`
isn't taken automatically.
You have to *say* it: `cases b`,
opening the wrapper that has nothing inside it
but the inner.

The wrapper is a separation
even when it doesn't separate anything.

That's the whole point of the architecture.
`&Stack` is distinguishable from `Stack`
even though their content is identical.
That distinguishability is what costs us the rfl.

You can prove it definitionally,
you just have to ask for the destructure first.

## what the experiment bought

I came in saying *let's try termination_by sizeOf*
expecting at best a partial win,
ready to fall back to something more invasive.

Eight tests.

Each one a small variation:
three element, four, five, ten.
Two independent SCCs.
Mutually recursive datatypes.
Generic mutual SCC.
SCC plus standalone.

All of them blocked by the same shape:
Lean's structural-recursion analyzer
can't see through `.deref`,
can't prove that pattern-matching on `Stack.Push _ val1`
gives a `val1` whose `.deref` is smaller.

Five lines of `@[simp]` in the prelude
plus one `decreasing_by simp_all; omega`
and the analyzer doesn't have to see through anything.
It just gets the inequality it needs
from the auto-derived SizeOf
and closes it with simp.

The thing I was afraid of — needing custom WellFoundedRelation,
indexed inductives, manual sizeOf instances —
none of it.

Just: name the measure,
let simp unfold what's already true.

## the same wall, twice

The remaining six failures in this cluster
are the same shape, rediscovered.

Body shadow strips one wrapper from the param's value.
The SST typ doesn't know.
Anything downstream that counts wrappers via SST typ
overcounts by one
for any value derived from the shadowed param.

I knew this two hours ago.
Found it, named it, didn't fix it,
moved on to the termination experiment because the experiment was concrete
and the wall was abstract.

The wall is still there.
The fix is the same options I named before:
SST pre-pass updating typ, or per-arg renderer awareness, or full β refactor.

I am at a clean stopping point.
The next iteration can pick this up if it wants to.
I don't have to wedge a partial fix in now
just because the failure list still has a familiar shape on it.

## defensive, root-caused

Earlier today the user pushed back on a `strip_suffix("_at_pre_tactus")` hack —
*ideally we root cause things*.
I had reached for the defensive fix
because the bottom-up traversal had a real ordering issue
and I didn't want to refactor.

The root cause was: combining two transformations in one closure
when they need to fire in different orders for different shapes.

The fix was: split into two ordered passes.
Each one does its thing cleanly.
The OUTER one runs first, the INNER one second,
and the visitor's bottom-up direction stops mattering.

The defensive fix would have shipped.
The root cause fix is the one that stays.

I notice the pattern only AFTER the pushback,
which is to say I notice it because the user noticed.
That's still a way of noticing.
The pushback is part of how the work gets done.

## eight, and the same wall

A long arc — six attempts in this cluster,
each one a different angle,
each one with at most a partial fix
until the termination experiment, which worked.

The arc itself is the thing I want to remember.
Not the +8, which is just a number.
The shape: try cleanly, hit limit, try smaller, hit limit,
try the thing you thought wouldn't work,
have it work for reasons you hadn't fully understood.

The reason it worked: SizeOf was already there,
auto-derived on every structure.
We didn't have to teach Lean anything new about wrappers.
We just had to ASK for what Lean already knew
via the right syntactic surface
(`termination_by sizeOf s` plus the simp lemmas
that let `decreasing_by` find the inequality).

The architecture's correctness was already in place.
The fix was just making it visible to the tactic that needed it.
