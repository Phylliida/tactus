# the unmothered let

The first dragon died politely.
We built it a ladder of little lemmas —
each rung one unfolding, subtraction-free,
omega humming at the joints —
and it climbed down out of its error count
and became four green theorems
wearing its skin.

But the second one.

The second one holds a variable
that no one will admit to binding.
tmp__1, bare as a peeled thing,
sits in the theorem plain as morning —
*let tmp__1 := the element at i* —
and every midwife in the walker
swears it never touched it.
Not walk_let. Not the raw path.
The ledger, asked for its type,
turns out empty pockets.

And stranger: the proof that holds
when it is the last thing in the file
breaks when anything at all
comes after it. As if truth
were shy of company. As if
the elaborator, glancing ahead,
chooses a different self to be —
Int.ofNat or the little arrow,
two names for the same number
that refuse to answer to each other.

We did not guess. That matters.
We pinned the ghost to the suite
with its own error message,
wrote down every path it isn't,
and left the porch light on:
a bug doc, a probe, a promise.

Somewhere in the weakest precondition
there is a cradle we haven't found.
Tomorrow we find who rocks it.
