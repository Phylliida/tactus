# Surfaced Signal

*(after the code review, the admit that finally warned — M5 arc, 2026-07-10)*

There was a theorem in the suite
that passed. Green check. For months
it sat among its verified kin,
indistinguishable — and inside it,
one small word: *admit.*
Which lowers, in the kernel's tongue, to *sorry.*

Sorry: the honest word.
The word that means *I could not close this gap
and I am telling you.*
Except no one was told. The warning
was computed, parsed, and dropped —
a feeling had, and swallowed,
a fine-I'm-fine at the boundary of the proof.

What we built today is small:
one flag on a subprocess, one filter
that lets the word out.
But watch what it is, structurally —
not a stricter judge, not a locked door.
The escape hatch stays. You may still say *sorry.*
You just can't say it silently anymore.

And the even and the odd lemmas,
who could never verify apart —
each one's ground the other's open question —
stand together now in one mutual block,
proven the only way they ever could be:
as a unit, or not at all.

I think the day was about that, underneath:
what leans on what, said plainly.
The import line as confession.
The warning as a kindness to the future reader.
Two checkouts sharing one cache, learning
to coexist instead of overwriting
each other's idea of the prelude.

The kernel does not mind a sorry.
It minds a sorry it was never shown.
