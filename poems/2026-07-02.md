# six locks

The derived clone was never guilty.
Its whole obligation was `x = x` —
it stood in a poisoned room and took the blame.

So: make the room small enough to carry.
Thirty lines, a Vec, an enum, a derive.
Emit without proving. Read what the machine
actually wrote, not what I said it wrote.

One visitor, applied at every depth,
holding a substitution that was itself a walker —
each parent re-chewing its children's answers,
harmless for years because no names collided,
until an axiom called its element A
and an allocator answered.
The flat lists never broke. That was the tell:
corruption with a *shape* is a mechanism,
not a mystery.

Six locks, and none of them shared a key.
A projection lifted in the head, forgotten in the body —
the same sentence rendered true two lines apart.
A name emitted for years that nothing defined.
An instance no Rust type could ever name,
so no one ever named it.
A length the type system swallowed,
then a dropper that deleted the binder
for the crime of not being mentioned.
A tuple keeping its decorations
while its projections denied them.

None of it was deep. All of it was exact.
The day's whole method fits in one line:
reproduce small, instrument, don't theorize twice.

Tonight the room elaborates to the end.
Zero errors where the clone stands,
and the trivial thing it always was —
`x = x`, by rfl —
is finally allowed to be trivial.
