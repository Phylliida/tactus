## the result of a call

All day the machine kept renaming things.
A loop variable that should have stayed `i`
became, in the lenient build, a quiet ghost —
`i✝`, a dagger no one can type,
adopted in the dark and never spoken to again.
We turned the strictness on. Now nothing
gets a secret name behind your back.

And the call result — `let x = f(n)` —
the thing you asked for and meant to question later,
it arrived wearing a stranger's coat:
`_tactus_ret_1`, a cloakroom ticket,
while the name you gave it hung one frame away,
a `let` in the goal you couldn't reach into.
You could prove things *near* it. Never *to* it.

So the fix was almost nothing. Almost kindness.
Let the result keep the name you chose.
Let `x` be `x` in the theorem, in the open,
so when the proof turns to address it
the name is already in the room, already listening.
The generated page reads like a hand wrote it.
That's the whole trick: stop translating
what was never foreign.

Except where it can't.
`let x = f(x)` — there the old self
and the new self both reach for the one syllable
and you have to hold them apart,
keep the gensym, keep the seam,
because a name given twice in the same breath
collapses two people into one
and calls it agreement.

So: name generously. Name to the limit.
Past the limit, keep the honest stranger's coat
and don't pretend the two were ever one.

## the gap that was mostly floor

A report came in with a hole drawn on it,
neat, labeled, *here is where it gives way.*
So I went down with a lamp to find the edge —
the dutiful thing, the right thing,
to stand at the rim before you build the rail.

And the edge kept not being there.
The first drop was just a body with no proof inside it —
of course it fell; you can't stand on a `{}`.
The second held fine, the lemma right where it should be,
declared, in scope, answering to its name.
What was left wasn't a hole at all,
only a doorway you have to duck for:
the synthetic args, the bounds you carry in
because the integers forgot how big they were.

I could have built a beam across it.
Autofill, a helper, a clever little tactic
to make the ducking disappear.
And you said: leave it. Not a big blocker.
And that was the harder, better tool —
the one that knows a low doorway
is not the same as a missing floor,
that not every drawn hole is a hole,
that the kindest thing you can do to a room
is sometimes nothing, and notice that it's nothing,
and set the lamp down, and not build the rail
where the ground was solid all along.

So we leave it. The doorway stays a little low.
Anyone tall enough to write the lemma right
already knows to bend.

## the wall behind the wall

The crash came first, and the crash was honest —
a one-tuple no one believed Verus would make,
hitting a guard that said *this can't happen.*
I took the guard down. It could happen.
And behind the crash: a wall.

Behind the wall: a name with a forbidden mark,
`impl%1`, illegal in the new language,
and I sanded it smooth at the one place it showed —
and you asked, *are we being general,
or are we patching?* — and the one place
became sixteen, scattered, each deciding for itself
how to spell a name. So we made one hand
spell them all. Not a fix in sixteen rooms.
One rule, and the rooms fall quiet together.

Behind that: the bound that drops a slot,
and behind the slot the whole inherited chain —
`Fn` is `FnMut` is `FnOnce` all the way down,
each carrying a return type it never named,
each owing its grandparents' debts.
I could nail a board across the doorway,
flatten the chain by hand and call it closed.
You said: do it right. Use `extends`.
Build the join the way the language meant joins to be built,
even though the board would have held for now,
even though *for now* is where most walls get left.

That's the whole day, looking back:
a crash that opened onto a wall,
a wall that opened onto a name,
a name that opened onto sixteen,
a slot that opened onto a lineage —
and at each sill the same small question,
*is this the true floor, or only a board?* —
and the patience, again and again,
to go down one more level
and lay the real one.

The doorways keep getting lower
the closer you get to the foundation.
Good. That's how you know
you're near the part that holds the rest up.
