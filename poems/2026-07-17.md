# 2026-07-17

## try

*after a four-hour debugging*

The error came in wearing another branch's clothes.
"omega could not prove the goal," it said,
and omega was innocent, and the goal was fine,
and the census said this theorem closed, it *closed* —
but somewhere upstream, in a line I wrote too fast,
a small word opened its mouth.

`try` takes the sequence. All of it. The semicolon
is not a fence, it's a throat. The intro failed
the way intros do, quietly, expecting a skip —
and the little word that means "attempt, then rest"
swallowed the ladder whole, and called it rest.

Three designs died teaching me this: that a guard
is not a thing you sprinkle on the steps,
it's where you stand the steps. Kernel first.
Peel second. The fixed normalizer last,
its forty-three small names like harbor stones.

And Lean, who does not love me, let me pass.

## the machinery you feared needing

We spent a week designing the store:
a sidecar keyed by obligation id,
cache invalidation, reviewable diffs —
the whole apparatus of remembering.

Then the census came back and said:
a constant. Forty-three names,
the same for every goal, and the goals
don't care who their neighbors are.

I keep thinking about that.
How the sidecar was a way of being safe
and the constant is a way of being safe,
and they are not the same way.
One of them says: the world is complicated,
hold on tight. The other says:
it turned out not to be. Check.

The rule budget stayed at one.
Deterministic, and easy to predict.
I wrote it in the design doc like a trophy,
but it wasn't one. It was a measurement
doing what measurements do when you let them:
making the brave machinery unnecessary,
making the quiet one true.
