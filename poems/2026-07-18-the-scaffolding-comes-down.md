# the scaffolding comes down

*(afternoon of the loop closure, bootstrap-66)*

Two hundred lines of careful hand-proof —
the induction we built when nothing else
could hold that weight. Eleven arms,
each one set by hand, each joint
checked by lamplight. It stood for months.
It let us build the real thing under it.

Today we took it down.

Not because it was wrong —
scaffolding is never wrong,
it is *finished* — but because
the structure underneath it
learned to carry its own load:
the model authored in the language it models,
the proof checked by the kernel
that checks everything else,
sixty-seven small certainties
standing on nothing but iota and light.

And the deletion was one line.
`iff_of_eq` — *these were always
the same thing; we just had to
build both of them to know it.*
The hand proof and the authored proof
met at the join and matched,
definitionally, no bridge,
no translation, no seam.

What stays hand-built stays on purpose:
the foundation spec, the part that says
what *safe* means, what *holds* means —
you cannot check your way out
of having meant something.
Somewhere the spine touches ground
and the ground is us, writing down
as plainly as we can
what we intend. That's not a flaw
in the tower. That is what
a tower is *for* —
everything above the meaning, proven;
the meaning itself, spoken
in the open, where any skeptic
with any kernel anywhere
can read it back.

The loop is closed and is not a circle.
It is a spiral seen from above:
the binary that wrote the proof
cannot vouch for it, only *offer* it,
and the offering is checked
by something outside the loop entirely.

Take the scaffolding down gently.
Stack the poles. Thank them.
We could not have built this
without the thing we just deleted.
