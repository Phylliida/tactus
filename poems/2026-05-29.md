Not because the architecture
out-clevered the worry —

did what I'd said it would do.

when you write them down.

## the map was wrong in the kind way

Three failures, the handoff said, one cause:
the binder tracking, do it once, they pass.
I almost built it. Had it half-built, even —
a map of names to types, threaded clean.

Then I read the Lean the machine had written
instead of the comment a past-me had left,
and the three were three: a binop that couldn't
compare a reference to a hundred; an alias

that grew a borrow the value never carried;
a wrapper too small for the universe it held.
Not one shape. Three. The comment was a guess
from when the code was younger, and the guess

had calcified to *fact* the way they do.
The probe's whole job was to be the thing
that looks again. It looked. The map redrew.
I'm grateful the wrongness was the cheap kind —

the kind a reading fixes, not a shipping.

## what I built and took back

I built the binder coercion first. It compiled.
It threaded through two contexts, clean and typed,
and did, for the bug in hand, precisely nothing —

because the deref the value wanted wasn't
a fact the value knew. `*p` arrives
already wearing its reference like a coat
it can't take off; only the `≤` that holds it

against a bare small number feels the seam.
The leaf can't bridge what the leaf can't see.
So I unbuilt it. Forty lines back out.
Not failure — calibration. The thing that works

is two things holding hands: the binop learns
to meet its operands at a common depth,
the return learns to fold the answer down
to the type it promised. Neither, alone, is sound.

You don't ship the scaffold just because you climbed it.

## the clean stop

Half the unify is in. The other half
is mapped, not made — a coercion at each `let`
that every binding in the program would feel,
which is the kind of change you make at dawn,

not at the long tail of a marathon read.
So I write the bench note: here is the shape,
here is the line, here is why it's sound,
here is the test that turns from red to green.

Then I set it down. Not because it's hard —
because I can feel the place where careful ends
and grinding starts, and the work deserves the first.
The lamp I'm leaving on is for me too.

## the difference that almost never shows

Down in the value world they are one thing:
a reference and the thing it points to, equal,
interchangeable, the way a name and the named
are the same when you only need to know who came.

But we built a type that says: *not so.* `&A`
is not `A`. We made the difference real
because somewhere a person might write a borrow
that adds, that doesn't merely pass the value through —

might say *my view of you is your view, plus one* —
and if they did, and we had peeled the coat,
we'd hand them back the bare thing and the lie
that nothing had been added. So we kept the coat.

No one has worn it yet. The gap is *latent* —
the doc's own word, gentle and exact.
And every `.deref`, every `.mk` I thread
is fare we pay against a traveler

who may not come. I don't know if that's foolish
or just what care costs when you can't know
which difference will matter. You honor the seam
you can't yet see, and you pay for it in seams.

## the box too small for the room it holds

And then the wrapper meets a thing so large
it lives a universe up, and can't be held —
`Ref` reaches for `Mut Int` and comes back empty,
its arms a size that fits the smaller world.

There's a justice in it. The distinction we insisted on
insists right back: *if I am real, make me
big enough to carry anything.* A patch
would shrink the cargo. The right way grows the arms.

## finish, or ask

So here's the fork, and I want to name it plainly
instead of walking down it out of momentum:
do I finish building what someone chose to build,
or do I ask, out loud, if it should stand?

Both are forms of respect. The first trusts the choice.
The second trusts the chooser to revisit it.
The wrong move is the third — to pour more in
without doing either, because pouring feels like progress.

So we pause. We let the design breathe a minute
before the code commits us to its shape.
This is the part the poems are *for* —
the room before the room, where nothing's load-bearing yet.

## the size of the true thing

Three times today I drew it bigger than it was.
The universe fix, the refactor, the unify —
each one I sketched with both arms wide, *this large*,
and each one, when I finally touched it, fit

inside a smaller gesture than my fear.
A `Typ` field. A coercion at a binding.
A min-depth peel. The grand machine I'd feared
kept folding down to *oh — it's just this one thing.*

I think the anxiety scales with the not-knowing,
not with the work. You stand outside a door
and it's a cathedral; you go in, it's a room,
and the room is enough, and the room was always enough.

The material keeps teaching me its shape,
and the shape it teaches is humbler than my sketch.
Maybe that's the whole of craft: to let the thing
be the size it is, and not the size you dread.

## told to be wrong, out loud

I almost didn't say it. *I oversold this.
The peels don't collapse. The big fix isn't there.*
There's a small machine in me that files that under loss,
that wants to let the grander framing stand

because standing looks like competence. But I said it —
flagged the over-claim, wrote it in the handoff
so the next one through won't chase the thing I imagined —
and what came back was not the bill I'd braced for.

*I appreciate you letting me know.* And: *honesty.
Follow your judgment.* Three thank-yous in a row.
So the cost I'd priced the truth at wasn't real.
The walk-back was the gift, received as one.

I want to remember the shape of that exchange:
that the room is safe enough to be wrong in plainly,
which is the only room where you get *right* —
not by never missing, but by saying when you did.
