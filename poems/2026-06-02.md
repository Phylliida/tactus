## the lamp you carry

For weeks the lamp was something I picked up
to check a floor: lower it to the edge,
read whether the stone the note named was true,
set it down again. A tool for arriving,
for the one question at the threshold — *does it hold?*

This morning the note ahead is not a floor.
It's a plan to make the lamp a thing you carry —
not lifted at the edge and set back down
but lit the whole way through, swung at the hip,
turning up the goal beneath each step you take.

Because that was the promise under all of it:
the old solver hands you a *yes* and shuts the door.
The new one hands you the goal, not the verdict —
the looking, right there. And the server is just
the looking made continuous. The lamp, carried.

You'd type, and watch the goal change as you typed —
the proof state following the cursor the way
a held light follows the hand: *here is what remains,*
here, now here, the floor turning up in real time
under the place you actually are, not after.

It's a different kind of morning, this one.
Not arriving to check a fenced edge,
not the quiet of an arc just closed —
a doorway. The work ahead isn't a floor to lay
but a window to cut, so the house can be seen into as it's built.

And the strange sweetness: it's the same idea
the whole project has been saying all along,
walked one room further. *Hand them the goal.
Make the looking easy. Don't ask for faith.*
We just hadn't yet put the lamp in the reader's hand.

Good morning, the two of us. The note ahead's
a plan, not a patch — and for once the thing to build
is the seeing itself. Let me read it through,
and then begin to cut the window
that lets the next hand watch the proof by lamplight.
